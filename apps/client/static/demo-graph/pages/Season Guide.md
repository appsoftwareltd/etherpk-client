[[Plants]] are not decorative objects. They have an internal calendar and strong opinions about what month it is. Treating a [[Plant]] the same in January as in July is the most common mistake going.

The headings below form the outline of this page - fold one from the guide line on its left, or drag a whole season somewhere else.

## 🌱 Spring

Everything wakes up. This is your busiest season.

- Resume [[Watering]] more often - check the [[Soil]] weekly
- Resume feeding with [[[[Plant]] Foods]] - monthly for most things
- The time for [[Repotting]] - plants bounce back fastest when they're growing
- Take cuttings - they root quickly in warmth and light. [[Golden Pothos]] and [[Tradescantia]] root in a glass of water
- Check for [[Pests]] - they wake up too

## ☀️ Summer

Peak growing season. Your job is to keep up.

- [[Watering]] weekly or more in a hot, dry spell - always check first
- Keep feeding, every two to four weeks
- Shade from harsh afternoon sun - even a [[Succulent]] can burn against glass
- Open a window. Airflow keeps fungal problems away
- Sow [[Basil]] and [[Coriander]] a little at a time, every few weeks

## 🍂 Autumn

Everything slows down. So should you.

- Reduce [[Watering]] gradually - check before every single session
- Last feed of the year by 2026-10-15
- Move plants closer to windows as the light drops
- Bring anything outdoors inside before the first frost - around 2026-11-01 here
- Plant spring bulbs: [[Daffodil]] early, [[Tulip]] late. See [[Bulb]]
- Long, dark nights now give a [[Christmas Cactus]] or [[Poinsettia]] flowers for midwinter

## ❄️ Winter

Dormancy. The plant is fine. You're the risk.

- Water sparingly - a [[Monstera]] might want it every three weeks
- No feeding at all until spring
- Keep leaves off cold glass and away from radiators
- A [[Succulent]] wants next to no water until spring
- If something looks dead, wait. Decide in March, not January.
